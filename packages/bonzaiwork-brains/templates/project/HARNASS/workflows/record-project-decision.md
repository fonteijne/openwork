# Record a project decision

## 1. Confirm decision status and authority

Determine whether the statement is a final decision, a proposal, or a discussion. Ask who made or approved it, when, its scope, rationale, alternatives considered, and any conditions or revisit triggers. Do not write an unconfirmed proposal as settled project knowledge. If authority is unclear, leave it as a draft for confirmation.

## 2. Find affected knowledge

Read `wiki/index.md` and search for relevant concepts, requirements, people, and existing decision records. Update the canonical relevant page where practical; do not create duplicate decision records. If the project has no decision-record convention, draft a clearly named `wiki/decision-<topic>.md` using the structure below and show it for approval before adding it.

## 3. Record decision context

Include:

- decision and status;
- decision-maker/approver and role (as supplied);
- date and scope;
- rationale and relevant evidence, linked to raw sources where applicable;
- alternatives considered, if known;
- implications, owners, conditions, and revisit trigger/date, if supplied;
- affected wiki pages and any prior decision superseded (without deleting it).

Keep facts, rationale, assumptions, and consequences distinguishable. Do not invent missing alternatives or rationale.

## 4. Human review and integration

Show the exact record and any edits to existing knowledge to the decision owner or user for approval before writing the decision as final. After approval, update the appropriate wiki page(s), add meaningful links and index entries where needed, and resolve a queue item only when the decision and rationale are recorded in affected knowledge.

## 5. Verify and report

Re-read changes, verify source links and index entries, and confirm earlier knowledge was not erased. Report the decision status, approver attribution, affected files, and unresolved conditions.
